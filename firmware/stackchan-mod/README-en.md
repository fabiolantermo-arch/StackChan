# Robottino MOD for Stack-chan — a prototype to be verified on hardware

> This page is the history of the first prototype (6–7 November 2026). The current behaviour is in `docs/architecture-en.md` and in the comments of the sources.

Upstream base: https://github.com/stack-chan/stack-chan — commit 906ee9a0cd2a0107a24c714efb10274f3b546afa, branch develop (not a stable release). Local copy of the sources in work/stack-chan-upstream. This module is outside upstream and uses its public APIs; it does not change the original firmware.

The face, updated on request: a stylised 3D female portrait, blonde with blue eyes. ONLY face and hair. Six 320×240 sprites: serene, happy, thoughtful, wink, small/large mouth. The Piu Content/Texture renderer driven by FaceState and the HTML preview share portrait-state.js. The mouth in the MOD follows mouth.open, not an oscillator; the HTML demo simulates the energy with an explicit checkbox. The old geometry.js experiment is not included in the MOD. The portrait looks straight ahead: there are no sprites with steerable pupils yet. Tracking moves the physical head. The generated pictures may differ slightly between frames; smoothness, rendering, memory and performance are to be tried on the hardware.

Movements: TargetTracker accepts normalised centroids 0..1, confidence >= 0.65, an increasing sequence and the target's id. It refuses NaN, replays and sudden changes of target. It stops sending poses after 1 s without detections. Maximum speed 0.65 rad/s, yaw ±0.8 rad (about 46°, widened by the user on 7 November 2026; the driver allows ±128°), pitch up to −0.52 rad (30° upwards, upstream convention: negative p looks up; the K151 servo uses −p limited to 5..85°, so from straight the head does not go down). Until 7 November 2026 the sign of the pitch was inverted. These are preliminary software limits, NOT a calibration of the K151. Movements start switched off; switching on from the menu needs a successful setTorque. Switching off keeps the pose; it does not release the neck abruptly.

BLE Local Peer: the official MediaPipe service tech.stackchan.demos.mediapipe. tracking.update events are decoded with the original vendored module (licence in vendor). Compatible at payload level with the upstream MediaPipe tool for face and hand; the module uses the face to follow and to mirror the elements of the face. It also accepts target.update with {id,x,y,confidence,sequence} for a separate object detector.

A generic object detector on the robot's camera is not yet implemented. A real source of the bounding boxes is needed: a vision engine on the device or processing on the phone. The receiver does not classify objects from images. The browser MediaPipe tool needs a device that runs the detection; it does not promise autonomy without that device.

Touch: touch-reaction.js and the active Piu renderer react to the release on the face with a local smile of about 3 seconds. The head sensor uses input.touchPanel.subscribe for press/forwardSwipe/backwardSwipe, already available upstream; we remove only our own listener, without closing or overwriting the host's sensor. Debounce 450 ms. It plays locally a short laugh generated with ElevenLabs' Sami, when audio is available, without sending requests or memories; the conditional movement is described below. It keeps the mouth.open state and goes back to the most recent base expression.

Dance: M5Stack's factory repository (different from the Moddable upstream) has DanceModifier::Happy/Robot/Panic/LookAround and the DANCE app over BLE. Reference pinned to commit 28b9f0c8bd6acd81cb3c70f1a93c3e373b39d400. dance.js is an original Moddable controller inspired by the Happy cadence 500/800/800/800/800/500 ms, with relative amplitude reduced to 0.15 rad and limits yaw ±0.8 / pitch ±0.3 (the same as following). It is not a complete port of the factory choreographies, LEDs or music. MIT in vendor/LICENSE-m5stack, included with the other licences in the artifacts.

Menu: switch movements on/off, short dance / stop dance, stop module. Never an automatic dance; it needs movements explicitly on and no pose in progress. During the dance tracking takes no targets; after the end or a stop it waits for a new detection. Serialised poses, transitions of 0.2 s and a step limited to 0.65 rad/s in software; on changing mode we do not update a virtual pose with the motors off. Stop dance halts the sending of new poses: the last transition already sent may complete, and it is not an emergency electrical stop. The face smiles without replacing the audio's mouth opening.

onUnload is exported for an explicit shutdown by a future loader, but it is not a hook declared by the upstream host: do not rely on it. Use Stop Robottino before replacing the module without a restart. A complete lifecycle will need a resource registration point in the host.

Robustness added before the hardware: a cancellable activation with invalidation of late confirmations, a single pose command in progress, and a 1200 ms watchdog on activation and pose. An unconfirmed command or a driver error blocks new movements until the module restarts, without ordering a jump to the centre or removing torque. It is not the same as an electrical stop or feedback of the physical position. After a restart the starting pose will still need verification and calibration.

Timing of the movements: the monotonic counter Moddable Time.ticks extended with Time.delta, rollovers of the native counter included; it does not depend on the civil clock. The tracker refuses invalid identifiers, non-finite or backward timestamps and replays. BLE subscription errors also release the subscriptions created in part. A face that has been disposed restarts no timer and ignores late touches or updates.

Current checks: 34 project tests + 17 MOD/face trials passed, typecheck and release build succeeded. They include touch, listener clean-up, speed and angles, stopping the dance, exclusion of tracking, cancelled activations, watchdog, drivers refused or failing synchronously, time rollover and the smile coexisting with mouth and wink. They are software trials with simulated capabilities, not tests of the motors, of Piu's graphics memory or of the physical camera.

Release build succeeded with the official Moddable SDK 10.0.2, commit 80076c48f5ab87ce29de728071b7408599071a12, installed only in work/moddable-sdk. Candidate target m5stackchan_cores3, not an attestation of K151 compatibility. Rebuild from the root with `node firmware/stackchan-mod/build.mjs`: it uses only the upstream `npm run mod:build` and copies archive and hash into firmware/artifacts. No flash; do not run `npm run mod` before confirmation. Six RGB565 textures take about 0.9 MiB plus any alpha and overhead; check the real size of the archive and the available XS partition. Building is not the same as running Piu on the device. Before installing: choose a compatible release, verify SKU/firmware/revision and partition sizes, calibrate the angles and ask for confirmation.

Preview verified in Chrome: expressions and the mouth checkbox work, no console error. Serve ONLY this folder, for example `python3 -m http.server 8767 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory firmware/stackchan-mod`, then open http://127.0.0.1:8767/preview.html. Do not expose the project's root (it contains private/).

Graphics: the built-in ImageGen tool, the reference is a picture from another project of the author. Prompt: a close-up portrait of only the head of a blonde, blue-eyed adult girl, a soft and friendly 3D style, blue background, six aligned frames with expressions, wink and mouth. Source in assets/girl-atlas-source.png; mechanical cropping and reduction reproducible with `node firmware/stackchan-mod/prepare-portraits.mjs`. The generator produced square cells, cropped to 4:3 without deforming them. No key or memory of other projects was copied.

The [Live link](../../docs/LIVE-IPHONE-ROBOT-en.md) now connects camera, I2S audio for the iPhone/robot call, head, face and display to the Cloudflare relay. The AI conversation is a distinct transport, still to be integrated into the MOD. The upstream example openai_live_webrtc is a base to evaluate, but it uses a WebRTC transport and input gating during output; it is not the same as our full-duplex bridge. Do not put the providers' API keys in the MOD. Lips from FaceState are prepared; synchronisation with our audio stream is still to be connected.

The Live module needs the documented native host modules and dedicated Preferences; a built archive does not prove they are present on the K151 firmware. Hardware verification and confirmation before flashing remain mandatory.

Touch update: a stroke on the head or a touch on the face show a smile for about 3 seconds. A small movement that returns to the previous pose is foreseen only with verified hardware, confirmed calibration, motors switched on locally and no remote command or dance in progress. Stop, switching off or an error interrupt the sequence without starting the return. The rendering of the smile, the sensors and the movement have not yet been tried physically.
