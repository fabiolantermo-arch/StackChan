# Technical decisions

*[Versione italiana](decisions-it.md) · English version*

Each entry: decision, reason, date. Add at the end, do not rewrite history.

## Infrastructure and security

- **Backend on Cloudflare Workers + Durable Objects** — the service must work without a Mac switched on; the DOs give state and WebSockets per user. (6 Nov 2026)
- **A project separate from the author's other projects** — no binding, memory or agent shared, so as not to mix data and risks. (6 Nov 2026)
- **Provider keys only in the Cloudflare secrets** — they must never reach the app, the firmware or the repository; the Worker acts as a relay and gets signed URLs. (6 Nov 2026)
- **Two distinct credentials (app and device)** — a compromised robot must not be able to act as the app, nor the reverse; `/api/robot/voice` refuses the app token. (6–7 Nov 2026)
- **Pairing with a 6-digit code instead of keys in the firmware** — the firmware stays publishable without secrets; the owner confirms from the app. A single-use code, 5 minutes, a block after 10 errors an hour; success only after `stored` from the robot. (7 Nov 2026)
- **The `verified` and `calibrated` switches turned on only by hand from the robot's menu** (a double press) — no movement until someone has really checked the hardware. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Deploy always with an explicit confirmation; autonomous git commits** — the user's choice: a deploy changes the real service, a local commit is reversible. (7 Nov 2026)

## Voice and conversation

- **ElevenLabs with the voice Sami as the default, OpenAI Live as the alternative** — the user's preference. (6 Nov 2026)
- **Web search: the tool `cerca_sul_web` for ElevenLabs, Responses delegation for OpenAI** — each provider with its own native mechanism; `responses_validation_failed` errors are not fatal. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Interrupting by voice with a volume threshold (`bargeLevel` 6000 RMS)** — the MOD cannot have C code, so no echo cancellation: while it talks the robot only measures the volume. To be tuned on the K151. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Mood and gestures from the text, with no extra AI calls** — keywords in the sentence; free and immediate. (7 Nov 2026)
- **The sad or happy face holds only for the sentence in progress** — the user: the sad face after "mi dispiace" stayed for the whole reply. The smile appears only with a real laugh ("ahah", "che ridere"…), not while listening. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Timers on the device, not saved as memories** — "ricordami tra…" is not a memory; the robot's timers are lost on restart (accepted). (7 Nov 2026)
- **The Test page works on the real robot** — the user: "I thought the test page worked on the real robot". It had remained a placeholder (a fixed text "Hardware non ancora verificato", a joystick only simulated). Now it reads the real state and, with movements on at the robot, the joystick moves the head through the Live relay, one command at a time, within ±10° (the limit of remote commands, not widened). (9 Nov 2026)
- **The robot reconnects after 1 s, not 5** — the relay closes the robot too when the app disconnects (a paired closing, for safety); with a 5 s wait a second choice of face right after the first did not arrive. First attempt after 1 s, then 2, 4… up to 60 s; the app waits for the robot up to 15 s. (9 Nov 2026)
- **The robot's mouth: a small picture over the serene face, four openings every 32 ms** — the user: "the movement of the lips is a bit choppy". Before, every change redrew the 320×240 points of the screen, with only three positions and a change every 97 ms. Now the Worker sends a pose every 32 ms with the same rule as the app (opening from the volume, 45 ms to open and 90 ms to close), the robot changes only the mouth's box as soon as the host updates the state (30 times a second) and the poses are the same cross-fades as in the app. The white robot keeps its two drawn mouths: a cross-fade would show two outlines. (9 Nov 2026)
- **Following the face with the robot's camera and recognition on the iPhone** — the user's choice among four roads (the iPhone as the eye, the robot's camera + iPhone, the robot alone simply, an Espressif recogniser in the host). The robot sends 160×120 images only when the previous one has left (since 10 November, once the face is found, small 80×60 images around the face: see "A small image once the face is found"); the iPhone (Vision) answers where the face is in that frame; the robot remembers the head's position for every frame and computes the goal from there, so a late answer does not make the head swerve. It needs the app open with Live connected. (9 Nov 2026)
- **The robot's voice in IMA ADPCM blocks played by the laugh's driver, the A-law microphone encoded by the host** — the robot's JavaScript is too slow to work sample by sample: decoding μ-law and measuring the mouth left the speaker without data (a choppy voice). The Worker sends `?audio=ima` (8.4 KB/s, half the μ-law) with the mouth levels already computed; the robot copies the bytes into the queue of the host's `pins/audioout` driver, which decodes and plays from a task of its own; the microphone uses the A-law encoding in C of `ChatAudioIO/Codecs`. The price: a slightly lower quality (about 22 dB of signal-to-noise ratio on the voice); if hiss is heard, the alternative is 16-bit PCM (32 KB/s), with the same scheme. The volume stays the provider's: beyond about −1 dBFS the amplifier (boost off) crackles. (9 Nov 2026)

## Faces and animation

- **Faces made of 320×240 pictures (six poses) instead of drawn faces** — more realistic; the pictures come from the user (ChatGPT) and are cropped by repeatable scripts (`prepare-redhead.swift`, `prepare-blonde-mouth.swift`). (7 Nov 2026)
- **While it talks only the mouth changes** — the generated "talk" poses also changed eyes and eyebrows, and the face jumped at every syllable. The serene face stays still and only a blended oval from the nose to the chin changes. Red: centre (165,193), 52×34. Blonde: centre (163,199), 40×26. (7 Nov 2026)
- **A continuous mouth in the app (`mouthOpenness` + `RobotMouthSmoother` + cross-fades)** — the user found the mouth "jerky". Fast opening (22/s), softer closing (11/s). On the robot three steps remain (`mouthLevel`), because the picture display does not cross-fade. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Following the face with the eyes, not by rotating the image** — more natural. Eye gain 2.5. When the face leaves, eyes and head stay in the last position and do not return to the centre, at the user's request. White robot: the whole eye moves with the eyebrow, not only the iris. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Irregular blinks (2.5–6.5 s, sometimes double) and a light breathing only in the app** — on the robot the "breathing" would leave a blue line on the hair. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Eye masks: in colour for the blonde, a drawn almond shape for the red** — on the red, skin and hazel iris have colours too close. (7 Nov 2026)

## Head and movement

- **Head limits: 46° right and left, 30° up, never down** (superseded on 9 November 2026, night: now 90° and 85°, see at the end) — the user's request, within the driver's ±128°. The K151 servo receives −p limited to 5–85°, so with the head straight it is already near the lower end stop. (7 Nov 2026)
- **A circular joystick on the Test page, returning to the centre** — it replaces the five buttons; it uses the same limits as following. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Limited dances (±10°, 1–32 steps, 60 s at most), validated twice (server and MOD)** — the servos' safety first. (7 Nov 2026)

- **The iOS simulator uses a camera lent by the Mac (local TCP 127.0.0.1:8765)** — the simulator has no camera. Apple allows only the iPhone's rear camera through Continuity. The code is built only for the simulator. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Interface texts at their original size** — the user tried larger ones and asked to go back. (7 Nov 2026)
- **Folders not renamed according to the watchos/backend scheme** — Xcode, Wrangler and the scripts depend on the paths; the map is in `CLAUDE.md`. (7 Nov 2026)

## Moddable host with a 6 MB `xs` partition (9 November 2026)

- The robot had M5Stack's factory firmware (XiaoZhi, not Moddable); the user chose to move to Moddable instead of adapting the service to the XiaoZhi protocol.
- The upstream host `906ee9a` needs Moddable 10.1.0 and ESP-IDF v6.1; the MOD too is built with 10.1.0 (`work/moddable-sdk-10.1.0`). Version 10.0.2 stays in `work/moddable-sdk` only as a reference.
- The standard table gives the MOD 256 KB; ours weighs 3.7 MB because of the face pictures. Instead of reducing the faces, a table of our own (`firmware/host/partitions.csv`): factory 9 MB (host 6.7 MB), `xs` 6 MB. The upstream tool that installs MODs reads the table from the robot, so it stays compatible.
- The upstream code is not changed: `firmware/host/build-host.mjs` adds a temporary manifest and removes it at the end of the build.

## No Claude Code notifications on the robot (9 November 2026)

- The user does not want balloons like "Claude Code ha finito" on the robot. The hooks were removed from `.claude/settings.json`; the script, the `/api/robot/reaction` endpoint and the handling in the MOD stay, but nothing calls them.

## Head lights: the "Discreta" scheme, "Media" brightness (9 November 2026)

- The user's choice among three proposals (Discreta, Espressiva, Solo spia) and two brightnesses (Tenue, Media).
- Off at rest, on only when the robot is doing something with the user: so, when microphone or camera are in use, there is always a light (it works as an indicator).
- Every colour change is an I2C write per LED on the PY32 (the same chip as the servos' power): the MOD writes only when the light changes and does not follow the voice, so as not to disturb audio and movements. If the LED driver fails, the lights stay still and the robot goes on.

## Calibration of the head in the MOD and movements switched on at start (9 November 2026)

- On the K151 the vertical motor refused the host's rest position (position 636): its reply carries the "out of limits" signal and the host takes it for the echo of the command and drops it (`protocols/scservo.ts` checks the fifth byte as if it were a command). The command stays without a confirmation and the MOD blocks the movements. Seen in the serial log: `[scservo] timeout id=2 command=3 address=42`.
- We do not change the host (it stays upstream). The MOD widens the host's limits and applies the centre and end stops measured on the robot; the servos' memory (offset, limits) is never written.
- Reading the positions does not move the head: the MOD gives the host a point to look at and removes it at once, so the host reads the servos a single time without moving them.
- "Calibrazione fatta" is turned on only at the end of the guided calibration; the old switch without measurements is turned off once. Dance from the menu only with verified hardware and calibration.
- The user's request: movements switched on by themselves at start, but only with verified hardware, calibration done and measurements saved; a choice made by hand in the first 2 s wins.

## Head from the iPhone up to 46° and 30°, the agent's greeting, Italian time, the red girl (9 November 2026, evening)

- The head command from the iPhone widened from ±10° to the robot's safety limits (46° to the sides, 30° up, never down): the user's explicit choice. Mirroring and dances stay ±10°. The MOD applies `safeRotation` to every pose and then the calibration; a long movement can last up to 2.6 s (maximum speed unchanged, 0.65 rad/s).
- An iPhone that disconnects after the motors have confirmed the pose is no longer a fault: the pose has happened. An unconfirmed command still blocks the motors.
- The calibration's trial covers all the reachable positions. If a motor refuses a position (a limit in its memory), the MOD shortens that side by 8 steps and tries again; a refusal at the centre, or more than 16, block the motors as before.
- Greeting at the start of the call: the user chose the agent's greeting instead of Sami's recorded greetings. Robottino's agent has no first message, so the Worker asks it to greet with a `user_message` right after the context, only for the robot's calls with ElevenLabs.
- Italian time in the MOD: UTC+1 and European daylight saving computed by the MOD (the host applies only a fixed time zone and started with Tokyo); the host's setting is saved as Paris (+1).
- The red girl: the original grid already cuts the bun at the top. Every expression now starts from the edge of the cell at scale 0.60 (before 0.67 and 50 points lower); app and robot use the same pictures, and the app's eyes (mask, position, band of the blink) follow the same transformation.

## Head up to 90° and 85°, a single speed, head straight at switching on, the face followed during the conversation (9 November 2026, night)

- **Range**: the user wants the head "to the right and to the left by 90 degrees, and up too". The MOD's safety limits (`HEAD_LIMITS` in `calibration.js`): 90° a side and up to 85° up (the host's maximum), never down; the real limit stays the calibration minus the margin. Worker, app and MOD accept commands up to 90°/85°; following with the robot's camera reaches the same limits. The "absolute" following over Bluetooth and the Studio simulation (`RobotHeadFollower`) stay at 46°/30°, and mirroring and dances at ±10°.
- **Calibration version 2**: the trial after the measurements reaches 85° up and 90° a side. A calibration saved before (tried only up to 46°/30°) is not used: the robot turns "Calibrazione fatta" off and asks for it to be redone, because a motor might refuse the new positions and block.
- **A single speed**: the user noticed movements sometimes fast and sometimes slow (a minimum of 0.3 s even for small steps, dances of fixed duration, durations computed from the last pose asked and not from where the head really was). Now every movement goes at 0.5 rad/s, about 29° a second (`HEAD_SPEED`), measured from where the head should be at that moment (even halfway through a movement), with a minimum of 0.2 s for small steps. The same speed for following, dances, gestures, commands from the iPhone and the calibration's trial. **Then (the same evening) to the maximum**, at the user's request ("raise the servo motors' speed to the maximum"): 3 rad/s, about 170°/s, with minimum steps of 0.1 s (one turn of the main loop). No further: the host passes the servo an arrival time, and if the servo cannot keep up, the position estimated by the MOD (`headNow`, used for the pose of every frame in following) would no longer be the true one. The dances have a slower pace of their own.
- **Head straight when the motors switch on** (at start or from the menu, only with verified hardware and calibration): the MOD reads the servos' position as during the calibration and takes the head to the centre at the single speed; without a reading the duration is the one for 90°, so never faster than usual. Reading and movement stay inside the 1200 ms watchdog.
- **A new attempt on the lost reply**: at 21:15 "Errore comando remoto" with the reason `scservo command timed out after 120ms` on a position within the range already tried. The MOD repeats the same command once when the host says "timed out" (the position must still be confirmed by the motors, and the watchdog stays); at the second failure the motors block as before. The attempt ends up in `calDiag` as `retry`. During the calibration's trial even a real refusal is tried twice before the limit is shortened.
- **Right and left** (the user's request: "it moves the opposite way"): first inverted in the app; then (22:10) the cause was found, the K151's horizontal servo turning against the convention, and the correction moved into the MOD (`YAW_SERVO`). The app sends the angles as they are.
- **"Muovi il robot vero"** reads the robot's state by itself: it is no longer necessary to press "Verifica il robot" first.
- **The face followed during the conversation, with the app open** (the user's choice): the robot says in the `hello` whether it is in conversation (`conversation`, sent again as soon as it changes); the Worker passes it in the state; the app in the foreground asks for the state every 4 s and, if the robot is talking with movements and camera ready, opens Live and starts following with the robot's camera; it closes it at the end of the conversation, with the app in the background or when a page (Live, Test, Aspetto, Studio) uses the link, because the relay accepts one app at a time. During the conversation the frames are sparser (400 ms) to leave memory, processor and network to the voice. A switch in the Robot tab, on by default.

## The voice in the feminine or masculine, frames in small pieces, movements that stay on (9 November 2026, night)

- **Gender of the voice**: the user's request, "the two girls must speak to me in the feminine… only the white robot in the masculine". Robot and app add `?face=portrait|white|redhead` when opening the voice; the Worker accepts only these three and adds a rule to the context (ElevenLabs) or to the instructions (OpenAI), repeated in the robot's opening greeting. Without a known face it adds nothing.
- **Camera: a single size, never together with the servos** (9–10 November 2026): the change of size made the host fall (`cameraLoop`); switching on and off keeps it busy for about a second. Video and following at 160×120; the camera switches on and off only with the servos confirmed and meanwhile no movement starts; following takes pictures with the head still.
- **The angle of view measured, not estimated**: 58° × 45° (`CAMERA_FOV` 1.01 × 0.79 rad), from the shift of the scene after known turns of the head.
- **The memory of following in the app** (9 November 2026): Vision works on a single 80×60 image and does not know the face has left. The app remembers the last sightings: half a face accepted and rebuilt (the width of the whole face seen in the last 4 s, or 0.64 of the height), a step towards the edge the face left from (two at most per loss), and the person seen from behind as a fallback. The values sent to the robot stay between 0 and 1, as the relay and the MOD want: the edge already makes the head turn by about 25°.
- **`socket.js` on the host's low-level WebSocket client** (9 November 2026): the host's web WebSocket restarted the robot when the service closed with data in the queue. The MOD cannot change the host, so it uses `device.network.ws.io` directly with the host's `device.network.wss` configuration (as the web WebSocket itself does): it sends only in the declared space, in pieces if needed, and after the closing drops the queue.
- **The camera paused during commands to the servos** (9 November 2026): with the camera on the robot was busy long enough to read the servos' reply late; the host gave it up for lost (120 ms), the MOD repeated and the 1.2 s check blocked the movements. Instead of lengthening the check (forbidden), the camera waits until the command is confirmed (`motionBusy`).
- **No glances at rest** (the user, 9 November 2026): at rest the head stays still; it moves only if commanded, to follow a face, for the gestures of conversation and alerts, or when touched.
- **Camera frames**: the host's WebSocket keeps the data not yet sent in the robot's fixed JavaScript memory. The robot now sends pieces of 8 KB, built one at a time, only when there is less than 16 KB in the queue, and one frame at a time (the camera's frame goes back to the driver after the last piece). Before, a 320×240 video frame queued all at once restarted the robot. Then pieces of 4 KB only with an empty queue (confirmations in time on the hotspot), and for following 80×60 grey images (kind 6, 4.8 KB instead of 38 KB): it is enough for Vision (confidence measured 0.67–0.72) and the head gets answers much more often.
- **The end of Live**: when the iPhone's link closes, the robot stops what the iPhone had started (following, dance) but leaves the movements as the user chose them; only the explicit STOP command turns them off. Before, it turned them off at every closing, and Test and following were then refused.
- **Face threshold for following**: 0.5 instead of 0.8 (`RobotFaceFollow.minimumConfidence`), because in the real images of the robot's camera, backlit, Vision gave 0.75–0.83.
- **Test page**: an automatic check on entering and on coming back to the foreground, without a "Verifica il robot" button (the user's request).

## Punctual confirmations on Live and the direction of the horizontal servo (9 November 2026, 22:10)

- The relay closes the link if a confirmation arrives after 1.5 s: the rule stays (confirmations are not weakened). It is the robot that sends the images in pieces of 4 KB only when it has nothing in the queue, so a confirmation waits at most one small piece. Measured on the hotspot: confirmations in 0.4–0.8 s, a 160×120 image every 1.5–2 s.
- The K151's horizontal servo turns against the convention of the host and the MOD (positive yaw = the robot's left). The correction sits in a single point, between rotation and servo positions (`calibration.js`, `YAW_SERVO = −1`): commands, following, imitation, dances and calibration stay coherent, and the calibration's measurements (servo positions) do not change.

- During following the pose goes to the motors only if it changes: before, the same pose left 10 times a second even with the face still.

## Connections and servos, the head looking at the user, the speaker closed calmly (9 November 2026, 23:40)

- **No movement while the robot opens a connection or switches the camera on**: the TLS handshake (up to 2.5 s measured) and switching the camera on or off (about 1 s) keep the robot busy; a servo reply read late looked lost and the 1.2 s check blocked the movements. Instead of lengthening the check (which stays as it is), the two jobs no longer overlap: `socket.js` says when a connection is opening (`connecting()`), `mod.js` sends no poses at that moment, Live and voice wait for the servos to be confirmed (1.5 s at most) before opening the connection.
- **Head up at the start of the conversation**: 25° up and straight, as the user asked; the conversation's gestures move around where the head is (before, a nod brought it back down); at the end of the conversation the head goes back straight.
- **The voice's speaker**: at the end of the conversation first `stop()`, then `close()` after 0.2 s, so that the host's driver does not deliver callbacks of an output already closed (an error in `deliverCallbacks`).
- **Head sensor and camera**: the host switches the sensor's panel off while the camera is on; the host is not changed, so at those moments the call is opened with a long touch on the screen.

## Following at full resolution and direct reading of the head sensor (10 November 2026, night)

- **A whole 160×120 grey image for following**: measured on the Mac with Vision on the real images of the robot's camera (the user's face shrunk as if it were farther). With 80×60 the face is found 5 times of 5 only within about 40 cm, 2 of 5 at about 60 cm, almost never beyond; with 160×120 5 of 5 up to more than a metre (a face of 15–19 pixels). Enlarging the 80×60 image in the app or sending 120×90 does not help. Cost: 19.2 KB an image instead of 4.8 (five pieces of 4 KB), so fewer images a second on a slow network; better few images in which the face is visible than many in which it is not. The pieces are converted from the camera's memory one at a time (no copy of 19 KB in the fixed memory) and one per turn of the timer (25 ms), so the robot is not busy for long. While the robot listens in conversation 80×60 stays: the microphone (16 KB/s) comes first. The Worker and the app already accepted kind 6 up to 160×120: no deployment. Since the morning of 10 November the whole image serves only to find the face: see "A small image once the face is found".
- **The head sensor read by the MOD too**: the host's recogniser gives a "press" when one of the three zones leaves 0 and nothing else until all are back to 0. The MOD reads the raw zones (`touchPanel.sample`, already read by the host every 50 ms, no extra I2C reading): a zone above 0 for 8 s makes the reading "stuck" and from there a finger is a rise above that level (a tap up to 0.3 s; a long touch from 0.9 to 6 s, given on release, because a level that rises and stays must not open a conversation). No writing to the sensor's registers and no switching on during the camera: the host switches it off on purpose and has a test for that. Temporary diagnostics: zones in the large menu's title, on the USB log and in `robottino/touchDiag`.
- **The blocking of movements**: stays as it is (the only way out is restarting the robot). An automatic recovery after a successful re-reading of the servos would be possible, but it changes a safety: only with the user's consent.

## Wi-Fi awake only while the robot transmits, the sensor's sensitivity left to the host (10 November 2026, morning)

- **Wi-Fi always awake during camera, following, Live call and conversation**: at rest the host keeps the radio in power saving (`min-modem`: it wakes about every 100 ms), and what arrives from the network (commands, replies, confirmations) can wait as long. **Correction (10 November, measured on the robot)**: at first this said that the sleeping radio was holding following to an image a second. It is not so: the speed is the same with and without the radio awake; the cause is the host's send memory (next section). The host already offers MODs a "loan" for this (`network-power`, `acquireLowLatency`, used by its sample MOD): while it is held the radio stays awake, when it is given back power saving returns. The MOD takes it with `lowLatency()` in `socket.js` only while something transmits and gives it back right after; if the module is missing or the driver refuses, everything stays as it was. Checked in the ESP-IDF 6.1 sources: with Bluetooth on, the request does not make the robot fall (at most it does less). An alternative discarded: keeping the radio always awake (it uses more at rest, for no reason).
- **The head sensor's sensitivity left at the host's level 3**: tried with the user at all levels. At 3 and 2 the fingertip is not felt, at 1 a zone gives a weak 1 now and then, at 0 the three zones go to 3 by themselves for seconds: the robot started conversations and laughed with nobody there. Better a touch that sometimes is not felt than a robot that laughs by itself; the long touch on the screen always works.


## A small image once the face is found (10 November 2026, morning)

- **Measured cause of the slow following**: the host's connection keeps at most 5.7 KB to send (5744 bytes, four packets: a setting of its build) and takes more only when the network has confirmed the previous ones, 130–190 ms later on the iPhone's hotspot. That is about 23 KB/s: a whole image of 19.2 KB a second. With and without the always-awake Wi-Fi the result is the same; the conversion to grey does not weigh.
- **A window around the face** (`FaceLock` in `tracker.js`, `windowChunks` in `live-wire.js`): when the iPhone has found the face, the robot knows its direction (the head's position of that frame + the point in the image) and sends only a 120×90 window around where the face should be now, shrunk to 80×60 with the mean of 2×2 pixels: 4.8 KB in three pieces of 1620 bytes, less than the send memory, so it leaves all at once. The robot brings the answer back to the coordinates of the whole image: app and Worker stay as they are. Measured on the Mac with Vision on the robot's real images: in the window the face is found from about 40 cm to 1 m with 2 pixels of error; closer, the whole image halved is needed (the same 80×60 format); taking a single pixel instead of the mean finds far fewer. Beyond a metre the window was not measured: if it is not enough, the robot goes back to the whole image by itself.
- **Which image to send**: the iPhone does not say how big the face is. If the first small images after a whole one stay without an answer the other size is tried (window ↔ whole halved); if neither works, only whole images for 10 s. The size that worked is kept; a failure is forgotten after 4 s (the person may only have moved away). An answer on the edge (0 or 1: a face cut or a search step of the app) is not a place to remember: the following image is whole, and on a window the head does not move. While the robot listens in conversation never the whole image: the halved one.
- **When the face is lost**: the iPhone answers only if it sees a face and to one image at a time; while its answer goes to the robot and the confirmation comes back (about half a second on the hotspot) it skips the images that reach it. So every answer restarts the wait: the face is lost when the first small image sent after the last answer has waited 1.5 s with no answer, or after 3 s with no answer at all. Measured on the robot: with 1 s counted from the last image sent before the answer the lock fell every three or four images even if every answer had found the face, and three images of eight were whole.
- **When to shoot**: the messages leave at once (`noDelay` on the socket: otherwise the last piece waited for the confirmation of the previous ones). The small image is taken when the connection has room for all three pieces (`SMALL_ROOM` 5000 bytes, or anyway after 600 ms) and at least 250 ms after the previous one. Tried on the robot, shooting as soon as there is room for a single piece: 2.0 images a second instead of 1.7 but no more answers, because the camera does not yet have a new image (it needs about 0.2 s after the previous one is returned) and the shot holds the whole robot still for up to 0.23 s. Removed.
- **Measured result** (22 s, a script on the Mac in place of the app): from 0.9 to 1.7 images a second; the small image arrives in about 70 ms instead of 0.6–1.1 s; the answers stay about one a second (the app looks at the following image only after the robot's confirmation, median 0.56 s), but each returns to the robot on average 0.65–0.87 s after the shot instead of about 1.3–1.5 s estimated before. It is the practical limit for the MOD on this link. With the real app and a face in front (the user, 10 November, 42 s from the USB log) the numbers are the same: 1.3 images a second, four in five small, 1.1 answers a second, each to the robot on average 0.75 s after the shot, none too late.
- **Alternatives discarded**: shooting on the arrival of every answer (a cycle of about 0.85 s, no better, and fewer attempts before losing the face); throwing away the whole image already on its way when the lock arrives; a lighter conversion (the time is not there); the app's answers without waiting for the confirmation (it touches the command → confirmation series of app and Worker). **Left to the user**: a larger send memory in the host (a new build and flash of the host), the home Wi-Fi in place of the hotspot.

## Doubled send memory in the host (10 November 2026: on the robot since 10:29)

- **Why**: what the robot manages to send is at most the connection's send memory divided by the network's round-trip time. With 5744 bytes and 130–190 ms on the hotspot that is about 23 KB/s. There are two roads: more memory (the host to be rebuilt and rewritten) or a shorter network (home Wi-Fi, never tried on the robot). The user asked to prepare the first.
- **How**: `TCP_SEND_BUFFER` in `firmware/host/build-host.mjs` (8 packets of 1436 = 11488 bytes), passed as `CONFIG_LWIP_TCP_SND_BUF_DEFAULT` with `esp32Config` in the temporary manifest. Moddable merges it over its own base values; Stack-chan's `sdkconfig`, merged last, has no network settings. Neither the SDK nor the upstream code is changed. The build reads the final `sdkconfig` and stops if the value has not arrived or if the receive window has changed; `build-info.json` records the three network values.
- **Why doubled and not more**: the memory is taken only by data in flight, but it is the robot's internal memory, which is scarce exactly while the camera is on. One step at a time: measure, and only afterwards decide whether to go higher (ESP-IDF accepts up to 65535).
- **Why only the send**: the limit measured was on the way out of the robot. The receive window (`CONFIG_LWIP_TCP_WND_DEFAULT`, 5744) concerns what the robot receives and stays as it is: one change at a time, so that any problem has a single cause.
- **The MOD does not change**: `socket.js` reads from the host how much room the connection has. The rules "a small image only with room for all the pieces" and "no queues" stay identical; with more memory they simply find room sooner.
- **Measured result** (10 November, the Mac's script, the robot on the hotspot): the connection takes up to 11.4 KB at once; colour video 0.9 images a second (about 35 KB/s) instead of one every 2 s or so; whole grey images about 1.4 a second, each in 0.4 s, instead of 0.9 in 0.6–1.1 s; with the face locked 2.3 images a second instead of 1.7, with the answers to the head still about one a second (there the limit is the command → confirmation round, not the sending). No restart or error in about 3 minutes of camera on. Conversation, Live audio and the real app still to be tried with this host.
- **Going back**: the host of 9 November is in `firmware/artifacts/host-2026-11-09/` with its SHA-256 values (before writing the new one, `verify-flash` confirmed it was identical to the one on the robot); the one of 10 November is in `host-2026-11-10/`. The partition table and bootloader of the new build are identical: only `xs_esp32.bin` at 0x10000 is written, and restored.
- **The build's trap**: Moddable's makefile copies its project template over the build folder when the template folder has a more recent date (it changes even without changes to the files), and so puts back its 4 MB partition table after `mcconfig` has written ours. `build-host.mjs` updates the build folder's date before starting; the check on the compiled table remains the real protection (on 10 November it stopped the wrong build before copying the files).

## Call audio in µ-law, a link that says why it closes (10 November 2026, afternoon)

- **µ-law at 8 kHz instead of PCM16 at 16 kHz**: the robot's link carries 30–35 KB/s in all and every message costs it about 10 ms whatever the size. PCM16 was 32 KB/s per direction in fifty messages a second; µ-law is 8 KB/s in ten pieces of 800 bytes. Microphone and speaker stay at 16 kHz on both sides: on the way out two samples become one with a small filter, on the way in the mean of two successive samples is put between them. Two marker bytes (B1 08) in front of every piece: what does not have them (the earlier format) is dropped, never played as noise. The rule is written twice, in the MOD (`live-wire.js`) and in the app (`RobotCallAudio`), with the same test values.
- **Measured result and limit**: with no incoming audio 93% of the robot's microphone gets through; with incoming audio only 22%. The two-voice call stays an open problem: it is no longer the amount of data, it must be understood what holds the robot still while it receives.
- **A session counted from when the app joins** (`src/robot-link.ts`, not deployed): the robot's link waits by itself between one session and the next and is renewed every 10 minutes; counting its age closed the app even after a minute. The 10-minute limit per session and the 15 s of silence stay.
- **The reason for the closing in plain words**: the relay closes with `robot_silent`, `app_silent`, `session_expired` or `command_timeout` (before `session_timeout` for three different things) and the app translates it (`RobotLiveClose`); an unknown reason is shown only if it looks like a relay reason.
- **The robot closes by itself after 12 s with nothing from the server** (`LINK_SILENCE_MS`): it sends a ping every 5 s and the server answers each, so silence means a blocked road. Before, it kept the microphone open even 70 s after the app had gone.

## No head commands while the audio changes; calm repetition (10 November 2026, afternoon)

- **What happened**: in the conversation started by the long touch the movements locked twice in a row («Errore del movimento», «Errore nella danza remota»), each time with one servo silent twice while the conversation was starting or switching to listening. On the Live page, without audio, the lost answers (about 4%) were all recovered.
- **Probable cause, read in the code and not proven on the robot**: the host waits 120 ms for the answer (`COMMAND_TIMEOUT_MS` in `scservo.ts`) and Moddable's loop serves the timers first and then the messages that have arrived; a JavaScript pause longer than about 115 ms across the command triggers the «timed out» with the answer already there. Opening and closing microphone and speaker, and opening a connection, make pauses like that.
- **Choice**: no safety is touched (a single repetition, never success without confirmation, the 1.2 s watch, a lock after two lost answers). Instead, command and pause are kept from overlapping: `headQuiet()` in `mod.js` holds every head command while the camera changes, a connection opens, the conversation starts or the audio has just changed (`voice.busy()`: 0.35 s after each opening or closing); the conversation starts after the confirmation of the pose in progress; local gestures start 0.3 s later.
- **Calm repetition** (`CalmWait` in `calibration.js`): the single repetition no longer starts at once, inside the same pause, but when three 20 ms timers in a row have arrived on time and `headQuiet()` is true, within 0.4 s in any case. It does not start after a STOP, after a lock or if the user switched the movements off in the meantime. An iPhone command refused because the audio is changing gives `motion_unavailable`, not a lock.
- **How it was tested**: only on the Mac. `soak` in `mod.test.mjs` runs the real `mod.js` in simulated conversations against a model of the servos and counts the violations (a command after a lock, two commands together, outside the measured limits, a third try, a command while the audio changes). With the old code the movements lock in 20 runs out of 20; with the new one in 0 out of 20 when the pauses come only from audio changes. The model's pauses are an estimate.
- **What it does not fix**: a servo that is really silent twice in a row still locks the movements, as it should. If after the trial on the robot the `retry e… g…` numbers show lost answers with the program on time, the way forward is in the host: a longer wait for the answer, and the check that today discards as «echo» every packet with the fifth byte at 2 or 3 (even a servo status with those values). That is a new build and a host write: only with the user's confirmation.
- **Imitation (smile, tilted head)**: set aside at the user's request; `tilt.js` stays out of the manifest.

## The host reads servo answers that have already arrived before declaring them lost (10 November 2026, 16:50)

- **What happened**: with MOD `2f4aeeb4` (no commands while the audio changes, calm repetition) the user's first conversation locked the movements again. The new numbers (`retry e393 g213`, `retry e563 g253`, `lost w145 e172 g180`) say the program was about 0.2 s late three times in a row: not isolated moments around audio changes, but repeated pauses for the whole conversation. Avoiding them one by one from the MOD is an endless chase.
- **Cause**: the host's driver waits for the answer with a 120 ms timer; the bytes stay in the serial port until JavaScript reads them, and Moddable serves timers before messages. After a pause longer than 120 ms the timer fires first and the answer that arrived in time is ignored.
- **Choice**: fix the driver, do not lengthen the wait. When the time runs out the line is read once (`PacketHandler.poll()`), and only if the answer is not there does the command fail. A longer wait would not have solved it (a pause longer than the new value would give the same error) and would have slowed down finding a servo that is really silent. The safeties stay identical: 120 ms, one repetition, no success without an answer, the 1.2 s watch.
- **How the change is kept**: `work/` is outside git, so the change lives as a patch in `firmware/host/patches/` and `build-host.mjs` applies it and checks that the upstream sources are «baseline plus patch», nothing else. The patch's fingerprint goes into the host's `build-info.json`.
- **Diagnostics**: `[scservo] late read id=… bytes=…` every time there were bytes to read when the time ran out. If after the fix `timeout`s still appear without program pauses, then the servos are really silent, and the check that discards a servo status equal to 2 or 3 as echo is what is left to look at.
- **Writes to the robot without confirmation** (user, 10 November, 16:51): MOD and host are written when the robot is connected; the checks (chip, table, a copy to go back to, fingerprints) remain, and the user is warned before erasing nvs or the whole memory.

## The speaker stays alive until its notices have arrived (10 November 2026, evening)

- **What happened**: the robot restarted during a conversation (18:13:55, `LoadProhibited` in `deliverCallbacks`, Moddable's `audioout.c`). The driver sends the program a message with the speaker's address for every piece of audio that starts; `close()` freed the memory without knowing whether a message was still queued.
- **Choice**: fix the driver (a patch to the Moddable checkout, in `firmware/host/patches/moddable/`), not work around it from the MOD. From the MOD it would mean stopping the speaker, waiting and closing at every change of turn, that is 0.2 s of microphone silence after every reply and timed logic that depends on the order between timers and messages, the same order that already gave the «lost» servo answers. In the driver a counter of the messages in flight and one extra reference held by `close()` are enough.
- **What stays in the MOD**: at the end of a conversation the speaker stops and closes 0.2 s later, as before; it does no harm and does not depend on the fix.
- **Limit**: the fix applies to ESP32 and to the explicit close; it was not tried on other platforms nor sent to Moddable.

## The 1.2 s watch looks at the servos' confirmation, not at the end of the promise chain (10 November 2026, evening)

- **What happened**: «Movimento non confermato» in a conversation with following on, without any lost servo answer. The numbers (`w1227 h792 g171`) say the command left for the host 0.8 s after the decision.
- **Cause**: on this host every promise step waits for one turn of the message queue (up to 0.4 s when the robot talks and follows). The pose went through two steps before `setPose` and six or seven after the servos' confirmation.
- **Choice**: do not lengthen the watch. The command leaves at once (`soon`), and the confirmation is the real one: the host calls the MOD inside the servo's answer (`onConfirmed`, a host patch), and the watch looks at that signal. With a host without the patch the signal arrives when the promise settles: later, but never a success without confirmation.
- **Why not change Moddable's promise queue** (emptying all of it at every message): it would touch the whole host, and a loop of already resolved promises would hold the program without limit. Only one thing was needed here: knowing in time that the servos had answered.
- **What stays slow**: until the end of the chain the pose stays «in progress» and the next one does not start, so under load following makes fewer corrections per second. To be measured on the robot (`pose slow h… c… e…`: c is the confirmation, e the end).

## Head sensor: level 1 with a filter tried, then level 3 again; tap up to the long touch (10 November 2026, evening)

- **Measurement** («Prova sensore testa», cable connected): nothing on the head = 0 at every level, even while the robot laughs; one fingertip = 0 even at the most sensitive level (less than 0.40% change in capacitance); the palm = 3 on all zones up to level 6 (over 3%). The signal depends on the surface laid on it: the sensor is made for a hand, not a finger.
- **What cannot be done**: make a single fingertip register. The chip's most sensitive level (0.40%) is not enough, and it is the one at which phantom touches appeared in the morning.
- **Choice**: level 1 (0.55 / 0.70 / 0.85%) and a reaction only to touches that reach 2 on a zone (0.70%). Compared with the host's level 3 (reaction from 0.95%) about a quarter less surface is enough: two or three flat fingers. The filter at 2 leaves a margin above the lowest threshold, where in the morning a zone gave a 1 now and then.
- **Tap up to the long touch** (`LongPress` in `voice-wire.js`): the host calls it a tap only up to 0.3 s, fingertip time; a pat with the hand lasts longer (measured 0.37 and 0.75 s). Everything released before 0.9 s is a tap, from 0.9 s it is a long touch: no interval in which a felt touch does nothing.
- **How to tell whether it must change**: every release writes duration and strength to the USB log. Readings with nobody near would mean going back to level 2 or 3; real touches with strength 1 would mean the filter is too high.
- **Result, 19:10**: at the second restart with level 1 the three zones read 2 and 3 by themselves for over a minute and a half and the robot started conversations with nobody there. The filter at 2 is not enough: the phantom state goes up to 3. **Back to level 3 at once**, where the same zones read 0. A robot that starts by itself is worse than a touch that needs the hand.
- **Final result, 20:41**: even with the protections, level 1 gave readings by itself (three zones together at 2 for 0.1–0.5 s, again and again) and the robot laughed and raised its head with nobody there. **Decided: the host's level 3 and that's it.** To try a lower level again one would first need to know where those readings come from (a long measurement with the robot still, with and without the cable, with and without the servos on), not more protections.
- **Tried again at 19:44 with the protections** (user's request): reset of the reference in the driver and in the MOD (at start-up, and after 4 s of readings above 0), a touch valid only from a clean reading, strength at least 2. Nobody knows why the phantom state appears, so the protections do not prevent it: they make it harmless and clear it. If a conversation still starts by itself, go back to level 3 (`HEAD_LEVEL`, `HEAD_GATE` 1) with no more attempts.
- **What would be needed to try again**: the per-channel reference reset (`Ref_rst1`, register 0x0A) exposed by the driver, used at start-up with the head still and every time a reading stays above 0 without changing; and a long trial with the robot still to see how often the phantom state appears. Until then the level is not touched.
